## What is the QUESTION and are there any issues with the QUESTION?
The question can be one of two things:
  1. Can Julia Gillard win the next federal election?
  2. Do you like the plan to remove a car lane of the Princess Bridge for sole use by cyclists?

It can be either question because this newspaper(?) does not make it clear for which of the two questions that visualization applies. I would assume it is most likely “Saturday’s Result”, though - but the problem is that the question isn’t clear.

The newspaper(?) should design the page to make it completely clear for which question the visual applies, such as by pointing an arrow from the question towards its visualization, or by drawing a polyhedron box encircling the visualization alongside its corresponding question.

## What is the DATA and are there any issues with the DATA?
The visualization does not completely match the data. 

The data in numbers shows that 90% of people voted no, while 10% voted yes… but the visualization is misleading, as the 517 “no” votes are in the smaller part of the colored in circle (making up about 20% of the circle, when it’s supposed to make up 90%), while the 53 “yes” votes are in the larger part of the colored in circle (making up about 80% of the circle when it’s supposed to make up 10%).

The 517 red “no” votes should switch places with the 53 green “yes” votes, as currently the visualization makes it seem like the 53 green “yes” votes make up the majority of the circle when the “yes” votes are the minority.

## What is the VISUAL and are there any issues with the VISUAL?
As stated under the response to the previous question, the visual is extremely misleading. The visual does not represent the data accurately as the part of the graph that is “yes” and the part of the graph that is “no” are not visualized correctly. The “no” part should be further filled in to actually fill up 90% of the circle. The red and green colors should continue to match what humans expect as well (red representing “no” and green representing “yes”).

## How to fix the key problems of this visualization:
  * Clearly label to which question the visualization applies (e.g. headline directly above the chart, using an arrow, placing the question and the corresponding visualization in the same polyhedron box)
  * Ensure the proportions of the circle accurately reflect the data (90% “no”, 10% “yes”)
  * Maintain intuitive color mapping (red = “no”, green = “yes”)
